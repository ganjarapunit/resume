Feedback vs Criticism
Feedback focuses on behavior and offers a path forward. Criticism attacks the person. Feedback says "Here's what happened and how to improve." Criticism says "You're wrong."
Psychological Safety
Tap to learn →
Psychological Safety
People can only receive feedback well when they feel safe. Build trust first, then deliver feedback. Without safety, even well-intended feedback feels like an attack.
Timing Matters
Tap to learn →
Timing Matters
Give feedback as close to the event as possible, but choose your moment. Never give feedback when either party is angry or stressed. A delayed calm conversation beats an immediate heated one.

🧩The SBI Model

A simple, powerful framework for structuring feedback: Situation · Behavior · Impact

S
Situation
Describe where and when the behavior occurred
Set the context by being specific about time and place. "During yesterday's team standup..." or "In Tuesday's client meeting..." This helps the person recall the exact event without feeling ambushed.
B
Behavior
Describe the specific behavior you observed
Stick to observable facts — not interpretations. Instead of "you were rude," say "you interrupted the client three times." Avoid judgmental language. Describe what you saw or heard, not what you assumed.
I
Impact
Explain the effect of the behavior
Share the impact on you, the team, or the outcome. "It made the client feel unheard" or "It delayed our timeline by two days." Impact creates the "why" — without it, feedback feels arbitrary.
✨ Example SBI Statement
"Situation: In yesterday's sprint review, Behavior: you interrupted Sarah three times while she was presenting her progress. Impact: It made her hesitant to continue sharing, and the team missed hearing about an important blocker."

🎭Scenario Practice

Read each scenario and choose the most constructive response.

Scenario 1 of 3
A team member consistently arrives 5-10 minutes late to morning standups, causing the team to wait or recap. How do you address this?
A "You're always late and it's disrespectful to everyone."
B "I've noticed you've been arriving after standup starts this week. Is everything okay? The team benefits from your input at the start."
C "Just so you know, standup starts at 9 AM. Try to be on time."
Scenario 2 of 3
A colleague's presentation included several factual errors that a client noticed. You need to give feedback before the next client meeting.
A "Your presentation had mistakes. Please fix them before the next meeting."
B "The client flagged three data points in your deck. Let's sit down together to review the sources before we resubmit — I want to make sure we're both aligned."
C "I'll just fix the errors myself this time."
Scenario 3 of 3
A junior developer wrote excellent code but their documentation is sparse, making it hard for others to maintain. You're their mentor.
A "Your code is great, but your documentation is terrible. Nobody can understand what you wrote."
B "Next time, please write better documentation."
C "Your implementation of the login feature is really solid. One thing that would help the team: adding more context in the comments about your logic. For example, explaining your approach to error handling would make it easier for others to build on your work."

📌 Key Takeaways

  • ✓ Feedback focuses on behavior, not the person — it offers a path forward
  • ✓ Use the SBI model: Situation → Behavior → Impact
  • ✓ Be specific, timely, and describe observable facts, not judgments
  • ✓ Build psychological safety before delivering feedback
  • ✓ Start with positive intent and offer support for improvement
