Read our model Done

3 min

Read one time. Then answer.

One clear theme in homework research is habit strength. Both Lee (2023) and Smith (2024) link short balanced tasks to stronger routines. Lee (2023) studied 60 students for two weeks and finds that brief everyday tasks develop better study routines. Students who did short 20 minute tasks each day scored 18 percent higher on a habit test. Smith (2024) adds that extended evening work harms concentration, which is linked to weaker habits. Students with long 90 minute late sessions reported lower focus. Together, these findings support short daily homework over long late sessions. While length alone does not explain everything, balanced length may build habits and protect focus for busy learners.

About 130 words. Same Lee plus Smith idea. We use this text all lesson. No new reading.

What is the theme?

Answer key

a. Habit strength. First sentence names it. Weather is never in the sources.

12

Plan with theme table Done

2 min

Choose one theme. Copy its row. Each number has a job.

My theme, choose one:

H row, what each number means: Who: 60 students joined. Time: study ran 2 weeks. Groups: A did short 20 minute tasks, B did long 90 minute late tasks. Test: same habit test at the end. Result: A scored 18 percent higher than B.

F row, same study, focus lens: Who: same 60 students. Time: same 2 weeks. Groups: same 20 minutes vs 90 minutes late. Test: same habit test, focus stayed fresh vs tired. Result: same 18 percent gap, plus lower focus for late group.

Answer key

Both good. H is easiest. F uses the same numbers with a focus lens. Example H: H, habits, 60 students, 2 weeks, 18 percent. Example F: F, focus, 60 students, 2 weeks, lower focus. Repeat the same theme noun in every line of your draft.
