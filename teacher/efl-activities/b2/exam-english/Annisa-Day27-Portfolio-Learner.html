Original 169-word model, available after your attempt

During a 30-day heat-exchanger audit at Pertamina RU IV, one safety question changed how I read a process report. I learned to treat a number not only as a result, but as a warning that may affect people, equipment, and the wider plant. My K3 safety certificate supported this lesson, but the strongest evidence came from using safety questions while reading real process data.

This experience leads to a wider argument. Safety should be a core thread in every engineering degree, not a separate topic students meet only at the end. If safety is connected to maths, chemistry, and design from the beginning, learners can ask what could go wrong and how a safer design can reduce the risk.

One limited counterpoint is that degrees already have limited time. The answer is not a long list of extra rules. Safety can be taught through real cases, lab decisions, and design reviews. Engineers create systems that affect real people, so safety must be part of engineering thinking from the start.

This is an original model blog opening, not a public learner artefact. Replace details with Annisa's real selected text.
