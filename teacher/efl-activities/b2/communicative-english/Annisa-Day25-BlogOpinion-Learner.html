Write the blog Complete

7 min

Plan for 1 minute. Write for 6 minutes.

Words: 0. Target: 180 to 220.
Original 213-word practice model, available after your attempt

Safety Belongs in Every Engineering Degree

Safety should be a core part of every engineering degree, not an optional extra. During a 30-day heat-exchanger audit at Pertamina RU IV, I learned that a small operating problem can affect people, equipment, and the wider plant. The technical report mattered, but so did knowing the K3 safety rules and asking the right questions. Safety changed how I interpreted the data. A number was not just a result; it could be a warning.

This experience leads to a wider argument. Students need to understand risk while they are learning design, not only after they enter industry. If safety is separate from maths and design, they may see it as paperwork. A core safety thread would connect each subject to three questions: what could go wrong, who could be harmed, and how can the design reduce that risk?

One objection is that degrees already have limited time. Adding more content could reduce technical design work. That is true. The answer is not another long list of rules. Safety should be taught through real cases, lab decisions, and design reviews, using knowledge students already study.

Safety belongs at the centre of engineering education because engineers create systems that affect real people. Teach safety as part of engineering thinking, not as a final administrative box.

This is original practice material, not a real published blog.
