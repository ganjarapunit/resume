Start

Say this at the start of the lesson:

Today I would like to practise a real business situation. Please correct me directly and help me sound more natural and professional. At the end, please give me five useful phrases and three things to improve.

Topic field for the EF app:

Role play: customer negotiation about price and value.

The same customer runs through Lessons 16 to 20: Weber, 250 kVA, a competitor that is cheaper, delivery in ten weeks. Lesson 19 you asked a colleague for help. Today you sit on the other side of the table and negotiate with the customer yourself.

How the 40 minutes work

  • Warm up and words, about 13 minutes
  • Concessions and language, about 12 minutes
  • Gaps and numbers, about 3 minutes
  • Negotiation role play, about 6 minutes
  • Full negotiation and homework, about 4 minutes
  • Three optional slides: written messages, numbers, fast listening

The one rule for today

  • Never drop the price without getting something in exchange
  • Ask what matters most before you offer anything
  • Give one concession at a time, small first
  • Close with a question, not a promise
  • Open the answer keys only after you have spoken
Answer key and models

There is no right answer on this slide. The tool you must leave with is the four move frame:

Frame: propose, justify, ask, close.

Propose an option, justify it with facts, ask what matters most to the customer, then close with a question. Every activity today builds one of those four moves.

Negotiation words, set B

Core 2

Objective Match five negotiation words to their meanings and reject two that are not needed.

Do: same task, harder set. Two meanings are traps.

Matched: 0 of 5

Answer key and models

6 flexible = L, willing to change something
7 in exchange = K, used when you give something and expect something in return
8 to finalize = J, to make the agreement final
9 sign-off = I, official approval from the person who decides
10 price review = H, an agreed re-examination of the price

Not used here: M, a first visit to check a site before quoting, and N, how long production or delivery takes.

Why these five matter: a negotiation is not closed until there is sign-off and someone finalizes it. Price review lets you give something today and still come back to the number later.

Quick say, stress and flow

Core 3

Objective Say the five negotiation phrases with clear stress and smooth linking.

Do: tap play, listen, then say it yourself. Calm and unhurried.

Answer key and models

1 ne-go-TIA-tion, primary stress on the last syllable TIA, the first two stay light
2 a con-CES-sion, primary stress on CES
3 meet HALF-way, stress on HALF at the start of the phrase, which sounds decisive
4 a PACK-age with full WAR-ran-ty, two content stresses, do not rush the warranty
5 FI-nal-ize to-DAY, stress on FI and DAY

Linking to practise: I could make ONE conCESsion. In EXchange, we could FI-nalize the packAGE toDAY.

Why it matters: a concession said quietly sounds like a retreat. Said clearly it sounds like a decision.

05

Which version would you say?

Core 4

Objective Choose the professional version of five negotiation lines and say why the other one fails.

Do: tap your choice, then explain the reason out loud before opening the key.

Answer key and reasons
  1. B, a small named reduction tied to a scope change, instead of a vague 10 percent
  2. A, could you asks and gives the customer the decision, Tell me takes it away
  3. B, it holds the price and offers something else, take it or leave it ends the talk
  4. B, asking for the target first keeps you from promising what you cannot give
  5. A, it asks before it commits, the flat version sounds like a refusal

The point: the wrong options are not ungrammatical, they are commercially dangerous. They give away margin or end the negotiation.

Your turn: rewrite 3A so the price still holds but the customer feels heard. Model: That is the best I can do on the number, and I can include a service upgrade at no extra cost.

06

The concession ladder

Core 5

Objective Rank five possible moves by what they cost you, and reject one move that is not a step at all.

Do part 1: give every move a rank, 1 cheapest for you and 5 most expensive. Mark the one that is not a step as none. Do part 2: say the ladder out loud, cheapest first.

Context: the customer needs about 10 percent less. You have room in warranty, scope and delivery, not much in margin.

Answer key and the reasoning

1 Confirm the delivery date and hold the price.
2 Add a service upgrade within the standard package.
3 Offer a small discount of about 2 percent on a shorter scope.
4 Offer a staged payment or a longer warranty.
5 Give a discount of 10 percent without changing the scope.
Not part of the ladder: promise a delivery date you cannot guarantee.

The rule: give the small moves first. Every step costs less than the one after it, and the customer usually stops at step 3 if you sound reasonable. Step 5 destroys margin for nothing.

The trap: promising a date you cannot keep is not a concession, it is a commitment you will break. In a real negotiation that costs you the next order.

07

Build the sentence, one extra word is a trap

Core 6

Objective Build five correct negotiation sentences and reject one extra word in each.

Do: tap the words in order. Tap the box to undo the last word. The dashed word is the trap.

Sentence 1 of 5

Answer key and models
  1. I could meet you halfway on price if we shorten the scope.
  2. Could you tell me which part of the package is least important?
  3. If you confirm today, we can finalize the quote and start production.
  4. We would need sign-off from our director before we can change the price.
  5. In exchange for a shorter scope, we can offer a price review after six months.

Why the traps fail: discount and urgent put pressure on you instead of on the scope, yesterday and guarantee promise something you cannot control, and putting customer into sentence 4 changes who has to approve.

Notice: every sentence gives something and asks for something. That is the whole negotiation in one structure.

08

The four move frame for a negotiation

Core 7

Objective Use the frame propose, justify, ask, close, and reject one line that does not belong.

Do part 1: put the four moves in order. Do part 2: choose the line that does not belong in a professional negotiation.

Part 1, order the moves

Part 2, which one does not belong?

Answer key and models

Part 1 order: 3, 4, 1, 2. That reads propose, justify, ask, close.

Part 2: the line You must give us a decision today or we lose the price. It is a threat, not a move, and it invites the customer to answer with their own threat. Everything else is a normal professional move.

Say it in one breath: I could offer two options. Both keep full warranty and 24 hour service, so the difference is in the scope. Which part of the scope would you change to make the budget work? If we agree today, I will send the revised quote and finalize it with your sign-off. Does that work?

09

Negotiation call, put the eight turns in order

Core 8

Objective Sequence a realistic price negotiation and read it with natural turn taking.

Do: order the turns, check, then read it aloud. Second time, no reading.

Answer key and structure

Order: 1-E, 2-G, 3-A, 4-B, 5-C, 6-D, 7-H, 8-F.

Structure to name out loud: acknowledge the gap, ask what matters least, let the customer name it, propose options, let them ask, explain the options, handle the halfway request with a condition, close with a question. Notice that the customer reveals the flexible item in turn A, and that turn F gives a concession in exchange for something.

Second read: retell the negotiation in five sentences of your own words, and name the one concession you gave.

10

Written customer messages into spoken English

Extension

Objective Convert blunt written messages from a customer into calm spoken responses.

Do: read the message, then build what you would actually say. Four separate messages. The chat shorthand is a trap.

Message 1 of 4

Answer key and models
  1. I understand the price is about ten percent above your budget. Could you tell me which part of the scope is least important, so I can check what is possible?
  2. If one year of warranty works for you, could you tell me which items you would drop with it?
  3. Eight percent on the current scope is not possible for us. Could I offer two options instead, so you can choose what matters most?
  4. Thanks for confirming. I will send the revised quote today, and we can finalize it after your sign-off on Friday.

Why it matters: customers write short and blunt because writing is cheap. Answering in the same tone loses the account. This is the email speaking skill from Month 1, lesson 6, now used under commercial pressure.

Item 3 is the hard one: you have to say no without closing the door, which is exactly what the two options do.

Spot the gap, three questions inside the negotiation

Speaking

Objective Ask the three questions that turn a price complaint into a real negotiation.

Do: choose the opener that fits each gap, then say the whole question out loud.

Known: Ms Weber says we are about 10 percent above budget, wants a shorter warranty, needs delivery in ten weeks. Unknown: the real budget limit, what she can flex on, who signs off.

Answer key and models

1 budget limit, opener What is. 2 what she can flex on, opener Could you tell me. 3 who signs off, opener Who.

Full models:
What is the actual budget limit for this project, so I can check what is possible?
Could you tell me which parts of the scope you could change: warranty, delivery or service response?
Who signs off internally on the price, and when do they decide?

Also acceptable: any three questions that name the missing item and stay calm. Correct the form, not the order.

12

Numbers in a negotiation

Extension

Objective Say four negotiation numbers with units and softeners.

Do: type a question or an offer for each number. Add about, around, approximately or could.

Answer key and models
  • Could you confirm the gap is about ten percent above your budget?
  • I could offer a five percent reduction if we shorten the delivery scope. Does that work?
  • Is the target around eighty thousand euros?
  • Could we confirm the delivery date is about ten weeks, and then look at two options?

Say, do not spell: ten percent, five percent, eighty thousand euros, ten weeks. Percent never takes an s.

Do not say: your price is too high. Say the gap is about ten percent above your budget. That sentence is a fact, the other one is an accusation.

Fast customer turn, summarise and respond

Extension

Objective Summarise a fast, firm customer position and reply with one clear negotiation move.

Do: listen at normal speed, then faster. Then summarise and reply. The transcript is below if you need it.

1.0x
Transcript of the customer turn

My budget is fixed at eighty thousand euros, and that is my final number. A competitor quoted seventy-two thousand with a one year warranty. I need delivery in ten weeks and a decision this week. If you cannot match it, we will go with them.

Answer key and models

Model summary: If I understood correctly, your budget is fixed at about eighty thousand euros, the competitor quoted seventy-two thousand with one year warranty, you need delivery in ten weeks and a decision this week. Is that right?

Model move: Could we compare like for like? Our scope includes three year warranty and 24 hour service response, and that is where the eight thousand sits. If one year of warranty is acceptable, I could offer two options today.

If you struggled: ask for the audio again more slowly. Do not ask a third time at the same speed.

14

Role play, the 90 second negotiation

Core 96 min

Objective Plan and run a short negotiation that gives one concession and closes with a question.

Step 1, plan silently for 30 seconds: your opening move, the one concession you would give, your closing question. Step 2: run the call, then tick the words you used. Five is a pass.

Case: Ms Weber, 250 kVA, we are about 10 percent above her budget, delivery in ten weeks. She opens with the budget number and asks you to match a competitor.

1Propose. Name an option, not a discount.
2Justify with facts: warranty, service response, delivery date.
3Ask what matters most to her, then listen.
4Concede once, in exchange for something.
5Close with a question and a date.

Tick the words you used:

Answer key and model run

You: Thanks for the call, Ms Weber. I understand we are about ten percent above your budget. I could offer two options instead of one number.
Ms Weber: We do not have time for options.
You: Then let me be quick. Both options keep the 250 kVA and delivery in ten weeks. The difference is warranty and service response.
Ms Weber: We only need one year of warranty.
You: That helps. Could you tell me which items you would drop with it, warranty on the alternator or on the engine?
Ms Weber: The alternator. Everything else must stay.
You: In exchange for the shorter scope on the alternator, I could offer a five percent reduction. Would that work for you?
Ms Weber: Possibly. Send me something today.
You: So I will send the revised quote with two options today, and you confirm sign-off by Friday. Does that work for you?

Pass mark: five or more target words, one concession that was exchanged for something, and a closing question. Tick the exact word you said, not the idea you meant.

Full negotiation under pressure, then takeaways

Task

Objective Complete a full negotiation with a director on the call, then save your takeaways.

Situation: Ms Weber says a competitor is 10 percent cheaper. Her director joins and says there is no discount without sign-off. You have today.

Speak for up to 3 minutes. Tick each point when you have included it.

Homework, choose one:

Save these after the lesson

5 corrected sentences, 5 new words, 5 business phrases, 3 errors to avoid. Write them in your notebook, not only in this file.

Answer key, what a complete answer contains
  • Never answers the price question with a bare number.
  • Asks the director or the customer what actually matters: budget, scope, warranty or date.
  • Gives exactly one concession and states what it is exchanged for.
  • Repeats the numbers correctly: 250 kVA, about 10 percent, around 80,000 euros, ten weeks.
  • Closes with a question and a date, so the other person has to answer.
  • Does not promise anything the director has not approved.

Five phrases to keep:
1 Could you tell me which part of the scope is least important for you?
2 I could meet you halfway if we fix the delivery date.
3 In exchange for a shorter scope, we could offer a price review after six months.
4 I could offer two options so you can choose what matters most.
5 If we agree today, I will send the revised quote. Does that work for you?

Three corrections to avoid:
1 You want too much, then Could you tell me which part of the scope is least important?
2 Ok, I give you 10 percent off, then I could reduce the price by 5 percent if we shorten the delivery scope.
3 That is my last price, take it, then That is the best I can do on price, and I can include a service upgrade.
