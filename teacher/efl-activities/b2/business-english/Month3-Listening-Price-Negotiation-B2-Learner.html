Month 3 | Sales negotiation

Price, competitor and value

Start here

You will listen to a real business call. Alex is a Sales Engineer. Mr. Keller is a customer who says the price is too high and that a competitor is cheaper.

Show transcript, text alternative for the audio

Alex: Good morning, Mr. Keller. Thank you for taking the time today. How are things on your side?

Mr. Keller: Good morning, Alex. Things are busy, but fine. Let's get straight to it. We received your offer, and honestly, the price is higher than we expected.

Alex: I understand. Price is always an important point. Before we go deeper, could you share what budget range you had in mind?

Mr. Keller: We were hoping for something closer to what competitor X is offering. They came in about 15% lower.

Alex: Thank you for being open about that. Can I ask what exactly competitor X is including in their offer? Sometimes the scope is not identical.

Mr. Keller: Their generator has similar power output. They also include installation and a two-year warranty.

Alex: That is helpful to know. Our offer includes installation as well, plus a three-year warranty and on-site training for your team. So, the scope is actually broader. Would it be fair to compare the total cost over the full warranty period?

Mr. Keller: Maybe, but my management is pushing me to reduce the purchase price. They do not look at long-term value.

Alex: I understand that pressure. What are the main decision criteria your management is using? Is it only the upfront price or also reliability and downtime?

Mr. Keller: Mainly price and delivery time. We need the unit running within eight weeks.

Alex: Understood. Delivery within eight weeks is possible from our side. Let me check with our production team and confirm exact dates. In the meantime, could you tell me if there are any technical requirements that competitor X might not cover, for example voltage stability or specific options?

Mr. Keller: We need stable output voltage under load. That is non-negotiable for us.

Alex: That is exactly where our voltage regulator comes in. It keeps the output voltage stable even when the load changes. This protects your equipment and reduces maintenance costs over time. Does competitor X offer a comparable regulator?

Mr. Keller: I am not sure. I would need to check their datasheet.

Alex: No problem. I can send you a short comparison sheet today, so you have both offers side by side. Would that help?

Mr. Keller: Yes, that would be useful. But I still need a better price, Alex. Can you do something on the price?

Alex: I hear you. I cannot promise a discount right now, but I can discuss options with my team. For example, we could look at a different configuration or a longer payment term. Would that be interesting for you?

Mr. Keller: A longer payment term could work. And if you can add the extended warranty at no extra cost, that would be strong.

Alex: I can check both points. Let me confirm with my colleague in pricing and come back to you by tomorrow afternoon. In the meantime, shall we agree on the next steps?

Mr. Keller: Yes. You send the comparison sheet and the updated offer. I will review it with my team and get back to you by Friday.

Alex: Perfect. To summarize, I will send the comparison sheet and the revised offer by tomorrow afternoon, including payment terms and warranty options. You review with your team and reply by Friday. Is that correct?

Mr. Keller: Yes, that is correct.

Alex: Great. Thank you, Mr. Keller. I appreciate your openness. Talk soon.

Mr. Keller: Thank you, Alex. Goodbye.

Audio for this activity

Show answers
  1. He wants to show the offers are not identical in scope. This shifts the focus from price to value.
  2. He is under pressure from his management and needs to justify any decision internally.
  3. He wants to understand what really matters to the customer before making concessions.
  4. He has limited authority on price and needs to consult his team. He avoids over-promising.
  5. He cares about long-term protection and total value, not just the upfront cost.
  6. He wants to confirm mutual understanding and avoid misunderstandings later.
  7. Alex sounds more in control. He asks questions, structures the conversation and sets clear next steps.

Block 5

Role play with your teacher

Situation: You are Alex, a Sales Engineer. Your teacher plays Mr. Keller, a customer. Mr. Keller says your price is too high and that a competitor is cheaper. Stay calm, ask good questions, focus on value, offer options and agree clear next steps.

Your instructions

  • Start with a professional greeting.
  • Ask at least two questions about the customer's needs and decision criteria.
  • Mention at least two advantages of your offer, for example warranty, on-site training or voltage stability.
  • Do not give a discount immediately. Offer options instead.
  • End by summarizing the next steps.
  • Try to use at least five useful phrases from this lesson.

Useful phrases: Could you share what budget range you had in mind? Can I ask what exactly is included? Would it be fair to compare the total cost? I hear you. I cannot promise a discount right now, but I can discuss options with my team. To summarize...
