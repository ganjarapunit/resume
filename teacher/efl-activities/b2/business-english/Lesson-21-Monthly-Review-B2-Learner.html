Answer key and models

1 objection = D, a concern the customer raises to block a sale
2 like for like = G, comparing two offers on the same scope
3 total cost of ownership = B, the full cost over the life of the asset
4 trade-off = F, what you give up to gain something else
5 cost-effective alternative = C, a cheaper option that still does the job

Not used here: A permission to reduce the price and E an agreed re-examination of the price. Both are in set B.

Say them: I understand the objection on price. Let us compare like for like. When you consider total cost of ownership, the scope is fair. Every trade-off is a choice. If needed, we can offer a cost-effective alternative.

Quick say, the phrases you will use most

Core 3

Objective Say the five most useful Month 3 phrases with clear stress and smooth linking.

Do: tap play, listen, then say it yourself. Calm and unhurried.

Answer key and models

1 com-PARE like for like, primary stress on PARE
2 the ob-JEC-TION on PRICE, primary stress on JEC, price is the last beat
3 a con-CES-sion, primary stress on CES
4 DIScount APproval, primary stress on DIS
5 meet HALF-way, stress on HALF at the start of the phrase, which sounds decisive

Linking to practise: Let us com-PARE like for like. Could I get DIScount APproval, and then we can meet HALF-way?

Why these five: these are the phrases that open, hold and close a price conversation. Everything else supports them.

05

Which version would you send?

Core 4

Objective Choose the professional version of five Month 3 lines and say why the other one fails.

Do: tap your choice, then explain the reason out loud before opening the key.

Answer key and reasons
  1. B, it keeps the price alive and asks for the number instead of guessing
  2. A, could you hands the decision to the customer, Tell me takes it away
  3. B, naming the two internal items makes the request concrete and easy to approve
  4. A, would you mind is the polite conditional, You must is an instruction to a peer
  5. B, comparing like for like is how you turn a price complaint into a fair comparison

The point: these are not grammar errors, they are commercial errors. At B2 the question is not is it correct, it is does it cost us the deal.

Your turn: rewrite 1A so it keeps the urgency and does not concede. Model: Could you tell me the target number, so I can check what we can do before this afternoon?

06

The whole customer case in seven steps

Core 5

Objective Put the complete Month 3 customer case in the order it really happens.

Do: order the seven steps, check, then say the whole case in one minute without looking.

Answer key and the real order

Order: 1-b, 2-g, 3-d, 4-e, 5-f, 6-a, 7-c.

In words: discover the requirements first, acknowledge the objection, compare like for like, ask internally, offer two options, meet halfway, close with sign-off.

Why discovery comes first: if you answer a price objection before you know the decision criteria and the budget, you are negotiating blind. That is the single most common sales mistake, and it is why Lesson 17 sits before Lesson 18.

Build the sentence, one extra word is a trap

Core 6

Objective Build five correct Month 3 sentences and reject one extra word in each.

Do: tap the words in order. Tap the box to undo the last word. Each line has one word too many.

Sentence 1 of 5

Answer key and models
  1. I understand your objection on price. Could you share the competitor offer?
  2. What are your key decision criteria apart from price?
  3. I need discount approval and the margin situation before I can confirm the price.
  4. When you consider total cost of ownership, our scope justifies the price.
  5. I could meet you halfway if we fix the delivery date.

The extra word in each line: 1 discount, 2 cheapest, 3 customer, 4 warranty, 5 yesterday. Do not name them before he answers. Ask: how many words did that line have, how many should it have?

Why the traps fail: discount and cheapest decide the price before you understand the scope, customer moves the approval to the wrong person, warranty promises a term you have not confirmed, and yesterday is simply not a word in a business sentence.

08

The eight moves, internal and customer

Core 7

Objective Match each line to the move it performs, across both frames.

Do: tap a line, then tap its move. Ten moves, eight are used.

Moves:

Matched: 0 of 8

Answer key and both frames

1 Thanks for calling back, Ms Weber. = A, acknowledge
2 Could you clarify what you need from me? = B, clarify
3 Could you send me a quote for 250 kVA by Thursday? = C, request
4 So the quote arrives Thursday and your answer on Friday. Is that right? = D, confirm
5 I could offer two options. = E, propose
6 When you consider total cost of ownership, our value justifies the price. = F, justify
7 Which part of the scope would you change to make the budget work? = G, ask
8 If we agree today, I will send the revised quote. Does that work for you? = H, close

Two frames: internal, acknowledge, clarify, request, confirm. Customer, propose, justify, ask, close.

Not moves: I, escalate and J, negotiate. Both are real words and both are real activities, but neither is one of the eight steps. Escalating is what you do when a frame fails.

The hardest pair: clarify and ask. Clarify pulls information towards you, ask pushes the decision towards the customer. Get that wrong and the call reverses.

09

The full case call, put the eight turns in order

Core 8

Objective Sequence the complete customer case and read it with natural turn taking.

Do: order the turns, check, then read it aloud. Second time, no reading.

Answer key and structure

Order: 1-c, 2-a, 3-h, 4-f, 5-g, 6-d, 7-b, 8-e.

Structure to name out loud: acknowledge the pressure, ask what matters least, let the customer name it, take it internally, handle the price request, propose two options, handle the last push, close with a date and a question. Notice that turn f removes the student from the call for a moment, and turn d is where the concession is exchanged for something.

10

She says this. What do you do next?

Speaking

Objective Choose the correct next move for four difficult customer lines.

Do: choose the move that comes next, then say the full line out loud before checking.

Frame: acknowledge, clarify, request, confirm for internal. Propose, justify, ask, close for the customer.

Answer key and models

1 acknowledge, then ask: I understand the budget is the issue. Which part of the scope is least important for you?
2 justify: When you consider total cost of ownership, our value justifies the price.
3 ask: Which trade-offs would be acceptable to you apart from price?
4 propose: I could offer two options so you can choose what matters most.

The trap in all four: none of them is answered with a discount. The instinct is to move on price, and price is always the last move, never the first.

11

Numbers across the whole month

Extension

Objective Say four Month 3 numbers with units and softeners.

Do: type a question or an offer for each number. Add about, around, approximately or could.

Answer key and models
  • Could you confirm the scope is 250 kVA with full warranty?
  • Is the competitor about 15 percent cheaper for the same scope?
  • Could you confirm the target is around 80,000 euros?
  • Is the lead time about ten weeks, including commissioning?

Say, do not spell: two hundred fifty kilovolt amps, fifteen percent, eighty thousand euros, ten weeks.

Never say: your price is too high. Say the gap is about fifteen percent above your budget. One sentence is a fact, the other is an accusation.

Fast case briefing, summarise and respond

Extension

Objective Summarise a fast case brief and respond with the correct first move.

Do: listen at normal speed, then faster. Then summarise and give your first move. The transcript is below if you need it.

1.0x
Transcript of the briefing

Weber needs 250 kVA with full warranty in ten weeks. The competitor is about fifteen percent cheaper but their warranty is narrower. Our budget is around eighty thousand euros and they need an answer today. No discount is approved yet, and the director signs off anything above ten percent.

Answer key and models

Model summary: If I understood correctly, Weber needs 250 kVA with full warranty in ten weeks, the competitor is about fifteen percent cheaper on a narrower warranty, the budget is around eighty thousand euros, they need an answer today, and nothing above ten percent is approved. Is that right?

Model first move: I understand the budget is the main issue. Which part of the scope is least important for you? I need that before I can check what is possible internally.

Why that move: asking costs nothing, and the answer decides whether this is a scope problem or a budget problem. Those need two different responses.

13

Ninety second case update

Production

Objective Give a structured 90 second case update with four signposts and no notes.

Do: speak for 60 to 90 seconds. The timer is optional and never penalises you.

1Case: who, what size, what deadline.
2Pressure: what the competitor does, what she wants.
3What I have done: discovery, comparison, internal check.
4What I need and next steps, with a date.
01:30
Answer key and model

The case is Weber, 250 kVA with full warranty, delivery in ten weeks. On pressure, the competitor is about fifteen percent cheaper but their warranty is narrower, and Ms Weber wants an answer today. So far we have established the decision criteria, compared like for like on total cost of ownership, and checked internally what we can do on discount approval and margin. What I still need is confirmation of the sign-off threshold above ten percent. My next steps are to send the revised quote with two options by Friday and call her back on Monday with the decision.

Listen for: the numbers, 250 kVA, 15 percent, 80,000 euros, ten weeks, and the two dates. If the numbers are missing, that is the single fix to give.

Full case simulation, the whole month in one call

Capstone5 min

Objective Run the complete Month 3 customer case using both frames and at least six target words.

Do: run the call without reading. Then tick the words you actually used. Six is a pass.

Case: Weber, 250 kVA, full warranty, ten weeks. Competitor about 15 percent cheaper on a narrower warranty. Budget around 80,000 euros. She needs an answer today. Nothing above 10 percent is approved without director sign-off.

1Acknowledge and ask. Do not mention price first.
2Compare like for like, then justify with total cost of ownership.
3State what you need internally and why.
4Propose two options and name the trade-off in each.
5Concede once, in exchange for something.
6Close with sign-off, a date and a question.

Tick the words you used:

Answer key and pass mark

Pass mark: six or more target words, both frames used at least once, exactly one concession, and a closing question with a date.

What a strong answer sounds like: acknowledge, ask what matters least, compare like for like on total cost of ownership, state the internal need, propose two options with a named trade-off, one concession in exchange for the delivery date, then close on sign-off and Friday.

What fails: three concessions, a bare discount, promising sign-off you have not got, or ending with I will think about it. That last one is the most common and the easiest to fix.
