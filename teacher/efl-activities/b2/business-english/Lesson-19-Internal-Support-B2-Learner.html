Start

Today I would like to practise a real business situation. Please correct me directly and help me sound more natural and professional. At the end, please give me five useful phrases and three things to improve.

Internal support: ask my colleague for help with pricing and options.

The same case runs through Lessons 16 to 19: customer Weber, 250 kVA, a competitor that is about 15 percent cheaper, and delivery in ten weeks. Today you stop talking to the customer and ask a colleague for help.

01

Warm up, five questions from Lessons 16 to 18

Review

Objective Speak five high value questions from earlier lessons without reading.

Do: read each prompt, then say one sentence out loud. Tick it when you have said it.

Answer key and models
  1. Could you walk me through the competitor scope so we compare like for like?
  2. What are your key decision criteria apart from price?
  3. I understand your objection on price. Could you share the competitor offer?
  4. When you consider total cost of ownership, our scope justifies the price.
  5. Who is the decision maker for final price approval?

Keep the softener. Could you, would you and may I are what make these professional. Without them you sound like an order.

Answer key and models

2 pricing request = E, a formal ask for prices or options
3 quote = A, the official price you send to a customer
4 lead time = B, how long production or delivery takes

05

Which version would you send?

Core 4

Objective Choose the professional version of five internal requests and say why the other one fails.

Do: tap your choice, then explain the reason out loud before opening the key.

Answer key and reasons
  1. B, a deadline and a softener turn a demand into a request
  2. A, the conditional makes it a question, the bare imperative sounds like an order
  3. B, it names the reason, so the colleague understands the priority
  4. A, would you mind is the polite conditional, you must is an instruction to a peer
  5. A, it sets a date and asks a clear question

The point: the wrong options are not ungrammatical, they are socially wrong. That matters more at B2 than another tense rule.

Your turn: rewrite 3A so it keeps the urgency and loses the bluntness. Model: I need the margin situation for a customer call today, could you send it before three?

06

Build the sentence, one extra word is a trap

Core 5

Objective Build five correct internal sentences and reject one extra word in each.

Do: tap the words in order. Tap the box to undo the last word. The dashed word is the trap.

Sentence 1 of 5

Answer key and models
  1. Could you send me a quote for 250 kVA by Thursday?
  2. We need discount approval before we can confirm the price.
  3. Could you check the internal policy on margin for this case?
  4. Delivery in ten weeks is fixed, so could you check the lead time?
  5. If the request is not possible, we can escalate it internally.

Why the traps fail: urgent and quickly add pressure and sound like commands, guarantee and warranty promise something you cannot confirm yet, and putting customer into sentence 3 changes who the question is about.

07

The four step frame for any internal ask

Core 6

Objective Use the frame acknowledge, clarify, request, confirm, and reject one line that does not belong.

Do part 1: put the four steps in order. Do part 2: choose the line that does not belong in a professional ask.

Part 1, order the steps

Part 2, which one does not belong?

Answer key and models

Part 1 order: 1, 2, 3, 4, which is acknowledge, clarify, request, confirm.

Part 2: the line Anna, I need the numbers now, so stop asking me questions. It is an order, it is aggressive, and it attacks the colleague instead of the problem. The other three are normal professional moves, and escalating is a legitimate last step when nothing else works.

Say it in one breath: Thanks for the update on the Weber case. Could you clarify what you need from me? Could you send me a quote for 250 kVA by Thursday? So the quote arrives Thursday and the approval on Friday. Is that right?

08

Full internal call, put the eight turns in order

Core 7

Objective Sequence a realistic internal call and read it with natural turn taking.

Do: order the turns, check, then read it aloud. Second time, no reading.

Answer key and structure

Order: A, B, C, D, E, F, G, H.

Structure to name out loud: open with a request, the colleague checks for more, the student gives the commercial reason, the colleague asks for the number, the student gives it, both agree a date, the student confirms. The reason comes before the number, which is why turn D exists.

Second read: retell the call in five sentences of your own words. That is the retrieval step that turns the frame into a habit.

09

Written notes into spoken English

Extension

Objective Convert informal written notes into professional spoken sentences.

Do: read the note, then build what you would actually say. The leftover text is a trap.

Note 1 of 4

Answer key and models
  1. Could you send me a quote for 250 kVA as soon as possible?
  2. Could we get discount approval for the Weber case?
  3. Could you share the margin situation for this case?
  4. Thanks, that is clear. I will update the customer on Friday.

Why it matters: chat shorthand is written language, so it has to be translated before it is spoken. This is the email speaking skill from Month 1, lesson 6, used in the other direction.

Item 4 is the hard one: thank you is fine on a call, but a professional close gives a next step instead of ending politely and doing nothing.

13

Role play, the internal support call

Core 85 min

Objective Run a full internal support call using at least five target words without notes.

Do: run the call in order, then tick the words you actually used. No reading.

Case: Weber, 250 kVA, competitor 15 percent cheaper, ten weeks lead time. Anna is busy and will ask: what exactly do you need?

1Open and acknowledge. Say why you are calling.
2Ask what Anna needs from you, then answer precisely.
3State the request with numbers: 250 kVA, 15 percent, around 80,000 euros.
4Handle the obstacle. If it is not possible, propose escalation.
5Close by confirming the dates in a question.

Tick the words you used:

Answer key and model run

You: Thanks for taking the call, Anna. Before I ask for anything, could you clarify what you need from me?
Anna: Give me the case, the number and the date you need it.
You: Weber needs 250 kVA with full warranty. The competitor is about 15 percent cheaper, so I need a pricing request and discount approval. My target is around 80,000 euros and I need the quote by Thursday.
Anna: Then I need the margin situation before I can approve anything.
You: Understood. Could you check the internal policy on margin and tell me what is possible? If the request is not possible, could we escalate it internally?
Anna: Send me the details and I will come back to you on Friday.
You: So the quote on Thursday and your answer on Friday. Is that right?

Pass mark: five or more target words, and the closing question. Tick the exact word you said, not the idea you meant.

14

Sixty second internal update

Production

Objective Give a structured 60 second internal update with four signposts and no notes.

Do: speak for 60 to 90 seconds. The timer is optional. Type a keyword to tick a signpost.

1Context: this is the case ...
2What the customer says: ...
3What I need internally: ...
4Next steps: ...
01:00
Answer key and model

This is the Weber case for 250 kVA with full warranty. The customer says a competitor is about 15 percent cheaper for the same power, and their warranty is narrower. Delivery in ten weeks is fixed on our side. What I need internally is a quote by Thursday, a margin situation, and discount approval if policy allows. My next steps are to send the full specification today, confirm the lead time, and come back to the customer on Friday with two options.

Listen for: the three numbers, 250 kVA, 15 percent, ten weeks, and the two dates, Thursday and Friday. If the numbers are missing, that is the single fix to give.

Full internal ask under time pressure

Task

Objective Complete a full internal ask with the frame, the numbers and a confirmed next step, then save your takeaways.

Situation: Anna is in a meeting. Her colleague says: I cannot approve a discount, the policy is not clear to me.

Speak for up to 3 minutes. Tick each point when you have included it.

Homework, choose one:

Save these after the lesson

5 corrected sentences, 5 new words, 5 business phrases, 3 errors to avoid. Write them in your notebook, not only in this file.

Answer key, what a complete answer contains
  • Acknowledges the problem without blaming the colleague.
  • Separates what Anna cannot do from what she can do, for example the quote or the lead time.
  • Repeats the numbers: 250 kVA, about 15 percent, around 80,000 euros, ten weeks.
  • Gives a real next step with a date and a named owner, not a general promise.
  • Confirms the date by asking a question, so the other person has to answer.

Five phrases to keep:
1 Could you send me a quote for 250 kVA by Thursday?
2 I need discount approval for the Weber case. Could we check the margin situation?
3 Could you clarify what you need from me to move this forward?
4 If this is not possible, could we escalate it internally?
5 So the quote arrives Thursday and the approval on Friday. Is that right?

Three corrections to avoid:
1 Give me a price, then Could you send me a quote by Thursday?
2 I need the margin now, then Could you share the margin situation?
3 If not possible, I will escalate you, then If this is not possible, could we escalate it internally?
