Vocabulary: stray
Part 1 • word meaning

Listen. Choose the meaning of stray as used in the recording.

Hint

Listen to Part 1 (0:00–0:45)

Answer key + transcript

Answer

B. In Harrogate the Stray is 200 acres of public open land. The recording says “looking across the hotel gardens to the picturesque stray beyond, upon which a unique game of cricket was going forward” — a separate open space, not the hotel garden or the pitch itself.

“She stood on the top steps of the Prospect Hotel, Harrogate, … looking across the hotel gardens to the picturesque stray beyond, upon which a unique game of cricket was just then going forward…”

5
Language: monopolise
Part 1 • vocabulary in context

Listen. The spiteful voice says Phinella “monopolises the best seat.” What does it mean here?

Hint

Listen to Part 1 (2:50–3:00)

Answer key + transcript

Answer

A. The complaint is that she “is allowed to monopolise the best seat as she does day after day” — repeated exclusive use, implying social favouritism.

“Box seat again!” snapped a spiteful female voice … “I wonder she is allowed to monopolise the best seat as she does day after day.”

Hint

Answer key + transcript

Answer

A. Tone (“snapped”), content (“I wonder she is allowed…”), and “as she does day after day” signal envy and an accusation of favouritism, not concern or admiration.

“Box seat again!” snapped a spiteful female voice behind her. “I wonder she is allowed to monopolise the best seat as she does day after day.”

10
Inference: character
Part 1 • implied meaning

Listen. What is implied by Phinella’s reaction — “The girl laughed … she swung lightly up to the perch”?

Hint

Listen to Part 1 (3:00–3:30)

Answer key + transcript

Answer

A. “Laughed” and “swung lightly” (with “a brief glimpse of … silken hose,” suggesting ease rather than hesitation) imply confidence and ease. No argument, hesitation or request for help is described.

“The girl laughed, as giving a brief glimpse of … she swung lightly up to the perch that was indeed wide enough to accommodate three persons…”
