Read Like a Pro, Teach It Like a Pro

Hi Anna. Fifteen short steps. Each step has two layers: first read as a pro reader, then bank how you would teach it. Speak first, then type to lock it in.

1Reader and teacher you

Sub-skill: readiness to read, personalising

Speak, two sentences each. Then type one line.

  1. What do you read slowly, what do you only skim?
  2. What slows your students most?
  3. One reading win from your classes this month?
Model: I read contracts slowly, news I skim. My students stall on long sentences and unknown words. One win: scanning for numbers worked well last week.

2Predict from the title

Sub-skill: predicting and previewing

THE SHALLOW CURRENT: Why Deep Reading Feels Harder Now

Speak your guess. What could shallow current mean? Problem, story, or advice? What three ideas do you expect?

Model: I expect a problem plus advice text about skim culture, with psychology and tips.
Teach it: title only, 30 seconds, three guesses. Do not confirm yet.

3Skim A, 90 seconds

Sub-skill: skimming for gist

A. We skim more than any generation before us. Feeds, headlines and summaries train the eye to move on quickly. This is useful for updates, but it weakens slow reading. Psychologists call this cognitive patience, the willingness to stay with a difficult page. When every text competes for a second of attention, patience thins. We start more, we finish less, and complex ideas feel heavier than they are.

Set 90 seconds. No dictionary. Gist only.

What is the main idea?
Teach it: timer on, eyes forward, gist in one line. Say: 90 seconds, no dictionary, one line.

4Scan B like a detective

Sub-skill: scanning for specific information

B. The problem is not intelligence. Studies of eye movement show skilled readers still make rapid jumps, but they return to hard spots with purpose. They slow at names, numbers and contrast words like yet, while and however. They ask, what is the writer claiming here, and where is the proof. Readers who pause at structure, first sentence, last sentence, linking words, recover meaning faster.

Scan for 2 minutes. Hunt, do not reread.

Teach it: questions before text. Say: read questions first, hunt, do not reread.

5Detail check

Sub-skill: reading for detail, True False Not Given

Look back at A and B. Decide with proof.

1. Feeds train the eye to move on quickly.
2. Skilled readers never make rapid jumps.
3. Eye studies were done only with students.
Teach it: verdict plus proof. If you cannot point to it, it is Not Given.

6Chunk one heavy sentence

Sub-skill: handling complex syntax

Readers who pause at structure, first sentence, last sentence, linking words, recover meaning faster than readers who reread every word from the start.

Where is the main verb?
Model: Strategic pausing beats full rereading.
Teach it: three slashes on board. Find the verb first.

7What does it point to

Sub-skill: reference and cohesion

Replace the small word. If sense holds, you are right.

Teach it: put your guess back in. Say: does it still make sense.

8Meet C, guess lexis

Sub-skill: deducing meaning from context

C. The answer is not to read everything slowly. Strong readers switch gears. They preview to build a map, skim to find the spine, scan to locate proof, then read one key passage closely. They accept some ambiguity on the first pass and resolve it on the second. Attention, like a muscle, responds to training.

Guess from context. No dictionary.

Teach it: clue, guess, check. Say: guess from friends nearby, check after.

9Where is the spine

Sub-skill: text structure and discourse signals

No new text. Map A, B, C.

A does what?
B does what?
C does what?
Teach it: first line, last line, links. Map in 60 seconds.

10Infer what is not said

Sub-skill: inference, implied meaning

  1. Why do complex ideas feel heavier than they are?
  2. Why does full rereading help less?
  3. Why accept ambiguity on the first pass?
Model: Skim habits thin patience, so effort feels like difficulty. Full rereading wastes time, pause at structure is faster. Early gaps clear once the map is built.
Teach it: what says it, what means it. Show both.

Teach it: vote with phrase. Say: worried, neutral or hopeful. Show me the words.

13Sixty second retell

Sub-skill: summarising and synthesising

Retell A to C for a busy friend. Include habit, skill, fix. Plan 30 seconds, speak 60 seconds.

Retell model: We skim constantly now, which helps updates but weakens slow reading. Skilled readers still move fast, but they slow at key signals and ask where the proof is. The fix is to switch gears, preview, skim, scan, then read one passage closely. Attention can be trained like a muscle.
Teach it: habit plus skill plus fix, one linker each.

14Teach it in 90 seconds

Sub-skill: modelling and checking, teacher clarity

Pick one move from today. Teach it aloud in 90 seconds as if to a B2 student. Name it, show it, check them.

Model: Today scanning. Watch me. Question first, hunt numbers and capitals. Your turn: hunt where they slow down. Show me.
Tip: less talk, more try. One glow, one grow after.

15Pro reader kit

Sub-skill: self regulation and transfer

Model: I will keep switching gears by purpose. I will teach preview first, title only, 30 seconds. My phrase is locate proof.
Next: one hard page, preview 30 sec, skim 1 min, close read one passage, 45 sec retell. Same for your class.
