Activity 1

Monday morning

Four messages land in your inbox on Monday morning. Read them and answer.

A. Can you send me the Q3 figures before the 10:30 call?

B. Is the report ready? I need it. Now.

C. Could you let me know whether the figures are ready?

D. Do you have the Q3 figures?

Model answer

C is the strongest first choice, because it names the item, is polite, and asks for a clear yes or no answer.

B is the one most people want to rewrite. It has no deadline, no "please", and the full stop after "Now" sounds like a threat. One workable rewrite: "Could you let me know if the report is ready? I need it before 10:30."

There is no single right answer here. Saying why is what matters.

Activity 2

Spot the problem

Six lines in this email thread have a problem with is, do, can or could. Write the correct version of each one.

Nadia: Hi Tom, do you have five minutes today? Tom: Sure. Are you be free at three? Nadia: Yes, I am. Where you want to meet? Tom: The meeting room on floor two. Do you can send me the draft first? Nadia: I'll send it now. Could you helping me check the numbers too? Tom: Of course. Do you need Priya there as well? Nadia: Yes, please. Could you ask her if she could join us? Tom: Sure. Last month I can't get into that room either. Nadia: Right, they changed the door code on Monday. Tom: Thanks. Do you have the new code? Nadia: Yes, I do. It's on the noticeboard. Tom: Are you sure it's working? Nadia: Yes, I do.
6. Tom asks: "Are you sure it's working?" Which reply is correct?
Model answer
  • 1. Are you free at three? An auxiliary does not take be after it.
  • 2. Where do you want to meet? A present simple question needs do.
  • 3. Can you send me the draft first? One auxiliary per question.
  • 4. Could you help me check the numbers too? After could, use the base verb with no to.
  • 5. Last month I couldn't get into that room either. Past meaning needs could.
  • 6. Yes, I am. The short answer copies the auxiliary you were asked with.

Activity 3

Sort and notice

Put these twelve sentences into four groups: is, do, can, could.

1.Is the client happy with the plan?

2.Is she working from home this week?

3.Is the printer still broken?

4.Do you know the deadline?

5.Does the system save automatically?

6.I don't have the file.

7.Can you cover the meeting on Friday?

8.Can I take a day off next week?

9.She can join after 3pm.

10.Could we move the call to 2pm?

11.We could offer them a discount.

12.Yesterday I could hear a noise from the server room.

Now answer these four questions.

a) Which group asks about now, or about this week?
b) Which group asks about habits or facts?
c) Sentence 7 asks whether the colleague is free to do something. Sentence 10 does what?
d) Which sentence is about the past?
Model answer

is: 1, 2, 3  ·  do: 4, 5, 6  ·  can: 7, 8, 9  ·  could: 10, 11, 12

  • a) is. Present continuous for an action now, and be plus adjective for a state.
  • b) do. Present simple for habits, routines and facts.
  • c) 7 asks about ability or availability, so can is asking. 10 offers an alternative, so could is suggesting. could is softer and keeps the idea in the shared space.
  • d) 12. Two signals: "yesterday", and could instead of can.

Activity 4

What is the job?

What job is each sentence doing? Choose the function for A to H.

A. Can you use the new CRM, or do you still need training?

B. Could you send me the link when you get a chance?

C. Can I open the window?

D. We could run the workshop in March instead.

E. Do you usually work late on Fridays?

F. Is the team ready for the audit?

G. Last year I couldn't present without notes.

H. The server looks fine, so it could be the network.

Model answer

A ability · B polite request · C permission · D suggestion · E habit · F now · G past ability · H possibility

Watch the traps. A looks like a request but it is about ability, because of the second half of the sentence. H looks like ability but it is possibility. D looks like possibility but "instead" makes it a proposal.

Activity 5

Build the question

Put each jumble in order to make a question. Two of them have a word you do not need.

Model answer
1.Where do you want to sit? ("the meeting" is extra)
2.Is the report finished yet? ("tomorrow" is extra)
3.Can she join the call on Friday?
4.Could you tell me when the deadline is?
5.Do you know if the client signed?
6.Can I take next Friday off?
7.Is he working from home today?
8.Could we move the meeting to Thursday?

Number 4 is the important one. After a question word in an indirect question, the word order goes back to normal: "when the deadline is", not "when is the deadline".

Activity 6

Short and sharp

Choose the reply that matches each question's auxiliary.

1. Do you have the Q3 figures?
2. Is the server working now?
3. Can you speak to the client this afternoon?
4. Are they ready to start?
5. Does Priya know about the change?
6. Could you finish the draft by Friday?
7. Could you use this software last year?
8. Can I take next Friday off? (Your manager replies.)
Model answer

1. Yes, I do.   2. Yes, it is.   3. Yes, I can.   4. Yes, they are.   5. Yes, she does.   6. Yes, of course.   7. Yes, I could.   8. Yes, you can.

The point in 6 and 7. In 6, could is making a request, so you are agreeing to do it now. Replying "Yes, I could" sounds like you are talking about a past or imaginary situation, so it sounds distant. In 7, could is past ability, so "Yes, I could" is exactly right. Same word, two jobs, two answers.

Activity 7

Complete the email

Complete Marco's email with one word for each gap.

Hi Lena, Sorry I missed your call. The training session (1) ___ still running, so my phone was on silent. (2) ___ you still need the figures today? If you (3) ___ wait until tomorrow morning, I'll send them first thing. (4) ___ I ask you to check the totals before I forward them? I (5) ___ always spot errors when I'm rushed. Also, (6) ___ we move our review to Thursday morning? I (7) ___ make Thursday afternoon, so that works better for me. Let me know if that (8) ___ possible. Thanks, Marco
Model answer
  • 1. is ongoing action now. An action in progress takes is, not do.
  • 2. Do present simple question to a colleague.
  • 3. can asking whether your colleague is able to wait.
  • 4. Could politeness. can would sound like a demand here.
  • 5. don't present simple negative, time marker "always".
  • 6. could suggestion, "we" keeps the decision shared.
  • 7. can't genuine inability, no past meaning and no politeness sought.
  • 8. is be plus adjective, so "do" is impossible here.

Activity 8

Then and now

Complete with can, can't, could or couldn't. The time markers are your clue.

Two blanks. The first is "could", the second is "can't".
Model answer
  • 1. a) could b) can't "when I was four" is past; "these days" is now and the meaning is inability.
  • 2. can't "Right now" plus a specific present failure.
  • 3. couldn't "last quarter" is finished past.
  • 4. can present ability, inferred from present evidence.
  • 5. could "Two years ago" is past, even though the second half of the sentence is now.
  • 6. Could past ability in the question.
  • 7. can't / can same person, same day, two different slots. The time marker decides.
  • 8. could possibility, not ability. The speaker does not know, and could is the hedge that keeps you from sounding certain.

Activity 9

Tone it down

Four people ask for the same report. Rank them 1 to 4, most direct to most polite.

A. Send me the report.

B. Can you send me the report?

C. Could you send me the report when you have a moment?

D. I was wondering if you could possibly send me the report at some point.

Which one fits a first email to a new client?
Which one fits a colleague sitting next to you?
Model answer

Ranking, most direct to most polite: 1 = A, 2 = B, 3 = C, 4 = D

A new client: C or D. You do not know this person yet, so you keep distance. A colleague next to you: B. D to a colleague you talk to all day would sound sarcastic or oddly cold.

E. Could you send me the password for the shared drive?
F. Could you tell me the client's phone number, please?
Your own: "Could you send me the file by 5pm?"

Activity 10

Turn it around

You want something. Ask for it. Write the question.

Model answer
  • 1. Where is the meeting? / Do you know where the meeting is?
  • 2. Has the client arrived? / Do you know if the client has arrived?
  • 3. Could you email me the minutes? / Can you send me the minutes when you get a chance?
  • 4. Is the printer fixed? / Do you know if the printer is fixed?
  • 5. Can I leave early on Friday? / Could I leave early on Friday?
  • 6. Could we move the workshop to the afternoon?

Direct and indirect versions both count. "Has the client arrived?" is a good option, because arrived pairs naturally with the present perfect.

Activity 11

Friday slot

You need 45 minutes with your manager this week to plan the Q4 project. Anything not listed as free is busy. The working day is 08:00 to 18:00.

Your diary

Mon 9:00 to 10:30 client call. Free from 14:00. Tue Offsite training, all day. Wed Interviews 9:00 to 12:00. Free 13:00 to 15:00. Review 15:00 to 16:00. Thu Free 9:00 to 11:00. Line manager 11:00 to 12:00. Free 13:00 to 14:00. Fri Free 8:00 to 10:00.

Your manager's diary

Mon In workshops 9:00 to 17:00. Tue Free 10:00 to 12:00. Free 15:00 to 16:00. Wed Out of office until 14:00. Free 14:00 to 17:00. Thu Free 8:00 to 9:30. Free 14:30 to 17:00. Fri Free 9:00 to 12:00.

Find the slot, then agree the time. You must use one question with is or are, one with do or does, one with can or could, and one could suggestion.

Model answer

Working: Wednesday 14:00 to 15:00, or Friday 09:00 to 10:00. Both diaries are free.

Why the others fail: Monday, your manager is in workshops. Tuesday, you are at offsite training. Thursday 09:00 to 09:30 is only 30 minutes, and Thursday afternoon has no mutual window at all, because your manager is not free until 14:30.

A model exchange:

L: Are you free on Wednesday afternoon? T: Yes, I am. From one until three. L: I'm back in the office at two. Could we meet then? T: Two works for me. Could we make it forty-five minutes? L: Sure. Say two till quarter to three. T: Does that work for your calendar? L: It does. Thanks.

Activity 12

The slipped deadline

You are two days late on a project for your biggest client. You have fifteen minutes with your manager. Your three options:

1. Ask the team to work the weekend.

2. Tell the client now and deliver the core part on the original date.

3. Ask the client for two extra days.

Prepare each option in one sentence. Then choose one and say it.

Model answer

1. "Could we ask the team to come in on Saturday?"
2. "Could we tell the client today and give them the core part on Friday?"
3. "Could we ask the client for two extra days?"

The commonly strongest answer is option 2, because clients forgive a delay far more easily than they forgive finding out late. If you picked option 1, think about this: everyone already worked a long week, and that may not be your call to make.

Activity 13

Write the email

Write 60 to 80 words to your line manager. Ask to move your one-to-one from Tuesday to a different day. Ask for a 20-minute slot. Mention one thing that has gone well.

Then check yourself.

  • Did I use all four auxiliaries?
  • Is every question in the right word order?
  • Is one question with could for politeness?
  • Have I avoided one auxiliary plus another?
Model answer
Hi Maria, I hope you're well. I wanted to ask whether we could move our one-to-one on Tuesday. Something has come up with the client, and I'm not sure I can give you my full attention that day. Does Thursday afternoon work better for you? If so, could I book twenty minutes, any time from two onwards? One thing worth mentioning: the new intake process is working well. The team are much faster at it now. Thanks, Punit

It uses all four auxiliaries: could (could move, could I book), can (I can give), does (Does Thursday afternoon work) and is (is working well).

If you wrote "Would Tuesday work for you?", that is a fine alternative too. The goal is choice, not one right form.

Activity 14

Which is better?

Two people want the same thing in the same office. Which line works better?

1. To a client you have never worked with:
2. To the colleague you sit beside every day:
3. To your director, to move a meeting:
Model answer
  • 1. b. A new client gets the polite version. You have no relationship yet, so distance is correct and safe.
  • 2. b. This is the one most learners get wrong, in the direction of over-politeness. To someone you talk to all day, the long version sounds sarcastic or unkind.
  • 3. b. It gives a reason, offers an alternative, and keeps the decision open. Version a gives an instruction to someone more senior than you.

Politeness is relational, not absolute. It is set by distance, hierarchy and how often you already speak.

Activity 15

Fix your own

These are lines from a real work email. Three are correct. Three have a problem. Which ones?

1. Do you know when the deadline is?
2. I couldn't attend the training last week because I was ill.
3. Is she be at the conference on Thursday?
4. Do you can send me the link?
5. Could we move the call to 3pm? I can't make 2pm.
6. Where you want to sit?
Model answer
  • 1. Correct. 2. Correct. 3. Has a problem: "Is she at the conference on Thursday?" An auxiliary already says be.
  • 4. Has a problem: "Can you send me the link?" One auxiliary per question.
  • 5. Correct. could for the suggestion, can't for the reason.
  • 6. Has a problem: "Where do you want to sit?" A present simple question needs do, and it goes before the subject.

Item 5 is worth pausing on, because it is correct and it does two jobs at once. Explain both halves and you have the whole system in one sentence.

Homework

Send a short message to a colleague, ideally a real one. Under 60 words. It must contain:

  • one is or are question
  • one do or does question
  • one can or could request
  • one could suggestion

Then rewrite the same message twice: once more formal, once more casual. Bring all three.

This targets the register problem from Activity 14, because it forces you to hold the same message at three levels of formality.
