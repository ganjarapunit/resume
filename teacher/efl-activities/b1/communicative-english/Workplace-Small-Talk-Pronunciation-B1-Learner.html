Welcome!

Today you will work on small talk at work. How to sound friendly with work friends.

We will work on:

* Strong parts of words

* Strong words in a sentence

* Voice up and voice down

* Words that join together

* Small groups of words

Example: "How ARE you DOing?" (voice goes up on "doing")

There are 15 activities. Take your time. You can check answers after each activity.

Activity 1: Listen and Notice

Listen first. Do not open the words yet. Two work friends talk fast.

Audio: ELLLO, Views #1034 Small Talk. Listen twice if needed.

What did you notice? Choose the best answer:

Edwin opens with "how ya going". What does he mean?

How does Edwin feel about winter?

Transcript (open only after listening)

Edwin: Hey Buddhi, how ya going? Buddhi: I am good Edwin, how are you? Edwin: I am not too bad. Did you do anything last weekend? Buddhi: Yes, I did. I went out with a bunch of friends after a very long time. Edwin: What did you guys do downtown? Buddhi: We went out for lunch, did some window shopping, then karaoke and dancing. Edwin: I spent about two hundred dollars on winter clothing. I prefer to sweat rather than freeze.

Answer Key

Q1: Their voices went up and down, and they sounded friendly

Q2: How are you? Edwin says it short and fast.

Q3: He does not like winter. He likes summer ("I prefer to sweat rather than freeze").

Key point: Fast talk: listen for short forms and feelings. You do not need every word.

Activity 2: Word Stress

One part of a word is strong. Which one is strong?

Answer Key

COLleague (first part strong)

PROject (a thing: first part strong)

COMfortable (first part strong)

Many learners say it wrong. Say it strong and right.

Activity 3: Sentence Stress

Important words are strong (like nouns and verbs). Small words are weak (like a, the, to).

Which words are strong?

Answer Key

Correct answer: "How ARE you DOing?" (strong: "ARE" and "DOing")

Why: "ARE" and "DOing" are important. "How" and "you" are small.

Key point: Weak small words = natural fast English.

Activity 4: Sound Friendly with Your Voice

Voice up = friendly. Voice down = finished.

Which question sounds friendly?

Answer Key

Correct answer: "Did you have a good weekend?" (voice goes up on "weekend")

Why: Voice up shows you want to hear the answer.

Key point: Voice up = warm and friendly.

Activity 5: Connected Speech

Fast people join words. Which one is right?

Answer Key

Connected speech examples:

"How are you" becomes "Howaryou" (join, no stops)

"What about you" becomes "Whaddaboutyou" (the t in the middle sounds like d)

Key point: Joined words are why fast English is hard. Good listening!

Activity 6: Small Groups

Speak in small groups. Stop at /. Which one is right?

Answer Key

Correct answer: "I went out with a bunch of friends / after a long time." (what + when)

Why: Groups follow meaning. First: what. Second: when.

Key point: Chunking helps the listener follow your meaning.

Activity 7: Shadow Reading

Listen to Todd and Edwin. They talk about weekend shopping. Repeat fast, right after them. Copy their music.

Audio: ELLLO, Views #1033 Weekend Wrap-up. New people. Same small talk.

Now type one short fast phrase you heard:

Model answers: "how you doing" (are dropped) | "a couple of friends" | "You don't strike me as the shopping type" | "a couple of hundred dollars" | "I don't want to freeze"

Transcript (open only after listening)

Todd: Hey Edwin, how you doing? Edwin: I went down to the city with a couple of friends. Todd: You don't strike me as the shopping type. Edwin: I bought a jacket and warm trousers, about a couple of hundred dollars. I don't want to freeze. We took the bus.

Answer Key

Target features:

* Short form: "how you doing" ("are" goes away in fast talk)

* Special phrase: "You don't strike me as..." (you do not seem like...)

* Strong: "COUple of HUNdred", "FREEZE", "WINter clothes"

Key point: Copy a new voice. Your ear learns fast.

Activity 8: Which Stress Is Right?

Which stress is right?

Answer Key

deVELopment (second part strong)

MEETing (first part strong)

COLleague (first part strong)

Key point: Strong first part = easy to hear.

Activity 9: Say It Strong

Listen to Tom and Martin. They meet. Then open the words below. Say each line 3 times.

Audio: ELLLO, Views #633 First Meeting. Good hellos to copy.

Type the line that is easy for you:

Transcript (open only after listening)

"Are you a teacher here?" (voice goes UP. Yes or no.)

"No, I am actually a graduate student." (strong: GRADuate, ACtually)

"My name is Martin." (voice goes DOWN. Hello.)

"Nice to meet you, Tom." (voice goes DOWN. Warm and nice.)

"Where are you from?" (voice goes DOWN. What/Where question.)

Answer Key

Target pronunciation:

* Voice UP: "Are you a teacher here?" (yes or no)

* Voice DOWN: "Where are you from?" (What/Where)

* Voice DOWN: "My name is Martin", "Nice to meet you" (hello)

* Strong: GRADuate STUdent

Key point: Good hellos start good talks.

Activity 10: Show Feelings with Your Voice

Your voice shows feelings. Which answer shows you are excited and happy?

Answer Key

Correct answer: "Wow! That's amazing!" (big happy voice going down)

Why: A big happy voice going down shows you are excited.

Key point: Your voice shows you care.

Activity 11: Build Your Dialogue

Finish the talk with me.

Dialogue framework:
A: "Morning! How are you?"
B: "_______________, thanks. What about you?"
A: "___________. Did you have a good weekend?"
B: "Yes, I went to _______________. It was really _______________."
A: "_______________! That sounds _______________."
B: "Thanks. Anyway, we should _______________."

Type B's first answer here:

Answer Key

Example completed dialogue:

A: "Morning! How are you?"

B: "Good, thanks. What about you?"

A: "Fine, thanks. Did you have a good weekend?"

B: "Yes, I went to a party. It was really fun."

A: "Wow! That sounds great."

B: "Thanks. Anyway, we should get to work."

Focus: Strong words. Voice up on questions. Small groups.

Activity 12: Coffee Machine Talk

You + teacher: talk at the coffee machine. Use:

* 1 hello (voice up)

* 2 weekend questions (strong words)

* 1 weather line (join the words)

* 1 goodbye (small group)

Start: "Morning! Did you have a good weekend?"

Ready? Click Mark Complete:

Answer Key

Expected language:

Opening: "Morning!" or "Hey!" (rising)

Questions: "How was your weekend?" or "What did you do?"

Comment: "The weather was nice." (weak "was" sounds like "wuz")

Closing: "We should get to work." (small groups: "get to / work")

Focus: Strong words. Voice up on questions. Small groups.

Activity 13: Tricky Moments

What do you say? Choose the best answer:

You need to go. Be nice. What do you say?

Answer Key

Correct answer: "I should get back to work. It was nice talking to you."

Why: Nice and warm. It gives a reason and a happy end.

Key point: A good goodbye keeps friends.

Activity 14: Record and Check Yourself

Use your phone. Record your voice. Say:

* "Morning! How are you doing?"

* "I had a really good weekend, thanks."

* "What about you?"

* "We should get on with the meeting."

Listen. Ask:

* Strong words OK?

* Voice up on questions?

* Small groups?

Type one good thing:

Answer Key

Check yourself:

* Strong words: can you hear them?

* Voice up: did questions go up?

* Small groups: did you stop at /?

Key point: Listen to yourself. You will get better.

Activity 15: Action Plan

Today: strong words, voice up/down, joined words, small groups.

Choose ONE. Work on it this week. Type it:

Tell your teacher: Where? How? What day do you check?

Answer Key

Example plan:

"This week, I work on voice up and down. I listen at the coffee machine. I copy my work friends. I check on Friday."

Key point: Small plans work. Do it every day.
