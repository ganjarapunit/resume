🧠AI Fundamentals

Artificial Intelligence
The broad field of creating machines that can perform tasks requiring human-like intelligence — reasoning, learning, perception, and decision-making.
Machine Learning
Tap to learn →
Machine Learning
A subset of AI where systems learn patterns from data without being explicitly programmed. The more quality data they get, the better they perform.
Generative AI
Tap to learn →
Generative AI
AI that creates new content — text, images, code, audio — based on patterns learned from training data. Examples: ChatGPT, Claude, DALL·E, Midjourney.
AI Limitations
Tap to learn →
AI Limitations
AI can hallucinate (make up facts), reflect training biases, lack real understanding, and become outdated. Always verify AI outputs before acting on them.

⚡Ethical AI Decision-Making

Read each scenario and choose the most responsible course of action.

Scenario 1 of 3
Your manager asks you to use ChatGPT to write a performance review for a team member. The AI has never worked with this person. What do you do?
Use the AI output as-is — it's probably objective and fair
Write a draft yourself, then use AI only to polish wording
Refuse — AI should never be used for HR purposes
Scenario 2 of 3
You're using an AI image generator for a client presentation. The output looks great but includes subtle gender and racial stereotypes in the people depicted. What do you do?
Use it anyway — the client won't notice subtle details
Regenerate with more inclusive prompts, review carefully, and manually adjust if needed
Avoid AI entirely and use stock photos instead
Scenario 3 of 3
A colleague shares a confidential customer list with an AI chatbot to "analyze purchasing patterns." Your company policy hasn't addressed AI data privacy yet. What's the right move?
It's fine — the policy doesn't say no, and the analysis would be useful
Flag it to your manager and privacy team — customer data shouldn't be uploaded to public AI tools without approval
Tell them to stop and delete it immediately — this is always illegal

📌 Key Takeaways

  • ✓ AI is a powerful tool but has real limitations — always verify its outputs
  • ✓ Good prompts are specific, contextual, and define the desired format
  • ✓ AI can reflect biases — review outputs through an inclusion lens
  • ✓ Never share confidential or personal data with public AI tools
  • ✓ AI augments human judgment — it doesn't replace it
